You can start a home-visit physiotherapy practice in India with your qualification, your state registration, a portable kit and a phone. What makes it last is everything around the treatment: clear consent and records, fees that account for travel, a sensible service area, personal safety, and a steady flow of referrals. This checklist walks through each step for a newly qualified or newly independent physiotherapist.

It is practical guidance, not legal or tax advice. Rules differ by state, so check with your state council and a chartered accountant before you finalise anything.

## Why start with home visits?

Home visits need far less money up front than a clinic: no rent, deposit or fit-out. Demand is steady in most Indian cities, driven by older adults who cannot manage stairs or traffic, patients just discharged after joint replacement, fractures or stroke, and families who prefer care at home.

The trade-offs are real too. You lose time to travel, you see fewer patients a day than in a clinic, you work without colleagues nearby, and you have less control over the environment. A good plan deals with each of these from day one.

## Are you registered, and can patients check it?

Registration is the first thing a cautious family or a referring doctor will ask about.

- **Register with your state council** if it already registers physiotherapists, and keep the certificate and number handy.
- **Follow the NCAHP rollout.** As reported, the Registration of Allied and Healthcare Professionals Regulations, 2026 require physiotherapists to register, with renewal linked to continuing professional development. Watch NCAHP and your state council for when online registration opens. Our guide on [NCAHP registration in 2026](/blog/ncahp-registration-2026-what-physiotherapists-need-to-know) covers the detail.
- **If your qualification is older or non-standard,** read the November 2026 reports on draft provisional registration regulations, which describe a grace period and a bridge-course route for some existing practitioners.
- **Keep association membership separate.** IAP membership is valuable, but it is not a statutory registration.

Put your registration number on your visiting card, your WhatsApp Business profile and every online listing.

## What should go in your home-visit kit?

Keep it light enough to carry on a two-wheeler or in a cab, and standardise it so nothing is forgotten:

- assessment tools: goniometer, measuring tape, reflex hammer, BP monitor, pulse oximeter, thermometer,
- treatment basics: resistance bands of several strengths, a small set of cuff weights, a stepper or small step, a balance pad, taping supplies,
- an electrotherapy unit only if you are trained and confident using it safely in a home,
- hand sanitiser, disposable towels or sheets, and a basic first-aid kit,
- printed home-exercise sheets or a way to send them digitally,
- your consent forms and a notebook or app for records.

Check every patient's home for a firm surface, enough space and safe flooring before you begin, and use what is there (a sturdy chair, a staircase rail) rather than bringing everything.

## How do you handle consent and records?

Home visits put you alone with a patient in their house, so documentation protects both of you.

- **Written consent at the first visit,** covering assessment, the treatment plan, hands-on techniques, fees and cancellation terms. For older patients, involve the family member who is making decisions, and record who was present.
- **Notes after every visit:** findings, what you did, the patient's response, home advice given and the plan for next time. Write them the same day.
- **Chaperones:** offer one for any intimate or sensitive examination, and note whether it was accepted.
- **Patient data:** the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified in November 2025, with consent-notice, purpose-limitation, retention and security obligations reported to apply after an 18-month transition. Small practices should already work this way: tell patients what you record and why, keep data only for their care, lock your phone, and do not leave reports in shared family groups.

### What about WhatsApp?

WhatsApp is how most Indian patients will contact you. Use a separate WhatsApp Business number, set working hours and an auto-reply, agree with the patient what you will send (exercise videos, appointment reminders, invoices), and save anything clinical to their record. Do not give medical advice outside your scope over chat; ask them to see their doctor when needed.

## How should you price home visits?

This post does not suggest a figure, because local rates vary widely between cities, and within a city between areas. Build your fee from the factors that matter:

- what a clinic session costs in your area,
- travel time and distance, including peak-hour traffic in cities like Bengaluru, Mumbai or Delhi NCR,
- the length of the session, and whether it includes assessment or a written report,
- your experience and any specialist training (neuro, cardiorespiratory, paediatrics, geriatrics).

Practical tips: define two or three zones by area or PIN code with one fee each; offer packages for post-surgical rehab only if you clearly state what happens to unused sessions; accept UPI and issue a simple receipt for every visit. Ask a chartered accountant about income tax and whether GST applies to your services, as treatment of rehabilitation services has varied between advance rulings.

## How do you stay safe and keep a sustainable day?

- Share your daily schedule and addresses with someone you trust.
- Cluster visits by area, and leave buffer time between them.
- Check first-visit requests before you go: a call, the referrer's name, or an address you can verify.
- Leave if you feel unsafe, and record why.
- Consider professional indemnity insurance from the start.

## Where do the first patients come from?

Most home-visit practices grow through referrals and visibility:

- **Doctors and hospitals.** Introduce yourself to local orthopaedic surgeons, neurologists, physicians and discharge coordinators. Send a short update to the referring doctor after the first assessment, with the patient's permission.
- **Past patients and their families.** Older adults talk to neighbours, RWA groups and family. Good care and punctuality do the marketing.
- **Being findable online.** Families search on Google and, increasingly, ask AI assistants for "physiotherapist home visit near me". A clear public profile listing your area, services, fees, languages and registration answers their questions before they call.

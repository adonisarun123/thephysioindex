Patients in India now find a physiotherapist the way they find a restaurant: they type "physiotherapist near me" into Google, check Maps, read reviews, or ask an AI assistant for a recommendation. To show up, you need four things working together: a complete and verified Google Business Profile, a steady flow of genuine reviews, clear text about your services somewhere Google can read it, and consistent listings on trusted directories. None of this needs paid ads, and none of it can be bought from Google. This checklist walks through each step.

## How does Google decide which physiotherapists to show nearby?

Google's Business Profile help says local results are based mainly on three things:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what the person searched for. A profile that clearly lists "physiotherapy", "home visits", "sports injury" and "post-surgery rehabilitation" matches more searches than one that just says "clinic".
- **Distance:** how far you are from the person searching, or from the area they named, such as "physiotherapist in Kothrud" or "home physio Velachery".
- **Prominence:** how well known your practice is, which Google says draws on things like reviews, ratings and links to your website.

Google also states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Be wary of anyone selling a "guaranteed top 3 on Maps" package.

## What should my Google Business Profile include?

Treat your profile as the front door of your practice. Google's own tips start with verifying the business and keeping information complete and accurate. For a physiotherapist, that means:

- **Verify the profile** so Google knows you are authorised to manage it.
- **Use your real practice name,** as it appears on your signboard and receipts, rather than a string of keywords such as "Best Physio Clinic Bangalore".
- **Choose the right primary category** (physiotherapist or physical therapy clinic) and add relevant secondary ones.
- **Set your address or service area.** Google advises a full address if customers can visit. If you only do home visits, follow Google's service-area guidance rather than showing your home address.
- **Keep hours accurate,** including special hours for Diwali, Pongal, Onam, Eid or Christmas closures. Wrong hours lead to missed calls and poor reviews.
- **Write a plain description** of what you treat, the languages you speak, whether you offer home visits and which areas you serve.
- **Add real photos** of your clinic entrance, treatment room and equipment. A clear photo of the building or lane helps patients find you in a crowded market area.
- **Add a working phone and WhatsApp number** that someone actually answers.

## How do I get more Google reviews without breaking the rules?

Reviews help patients choose and contribute to prominence. Google's policy, though, is strict: reviews influenced by a payment, discount, free session or any other benefit count as incentivised reviews and are not allowed. Reviews biased by a personal or professional conflict of interest, for example from your own staff or relatives, are also covered.

What works, and stays within the rules:

- **Ask every patient at a natural moment,** such as discharge or a good progress review, not just the ones you expect to be happy.
- **Make it easy.** Send the review link from your profile on WhatsApp after the session, with a short, polite message.
- **Reply to every review,** thanking people and responding calmly to criticism. Google's tips say replying shows you value feedback. Never confirm in a reply that someone is your patient or mention their condition; keep it general.
- **Never write reviews yourself,** buy them, or swap them with other clinics.

## Do I need a website?

You can start without one, but a simple website gives Google, patients and AI tools more to read. A single, fast page is enough if it covers who you are, your qualifications and registration, conditions you commonly treat, your clinic address or service areas, fees or fee ranges, languages and how to book.

Write in plain sentences, not images of text. A patient in Lucknow or Kochi is searching in everyday words, so "help after knee replacement at home" is more useful than "comprehensive post-operative orthopaedic rehabilitation solutions".

## How do AI assistants decide which physiotherapist to mention?

More patients now ask tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Mode questions like "Which physiotherapist in Hyderabad does home visits for stroke rehab?". Each tool works differently, but they draw on what is publicly available on the web.

For Google's own AI features, Google Search Central is direct: there are no extra requirements and no special optimisation needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. A page needs to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet. Its recommended practices are the familiar ones: let search engines crawl your pages, keep important content in text, link your pages together, add good photos, make any structured data match what is on the page, and keep your Business Profile up to date.

In practice, the same habits that help Google Maps also help AI assistants:

- **Be consistent everywhere.** Use the same practice name, address or service area, phone number and services on Google, your website, social media and every directory you appear on.
- **Be specific.** State the conditions you treat, the areas you cover and whether you offer home visits.
- **Show trust signals.** Qualifications, council registration and years in practice help patients, and any tool summarising you, understand who you are.
- **Keep it current.** Update fees, hours and areas when they change.

## Where do directories fit in?

Directories give patients, and the tools that read the web, another trustworthy source that confirms your details. A good directory profile is structured, shows the same facts as your Google profile and links patients straight to you without a middleman taking a cut.
